# Religious Question, Fatwa and Amal/Wazifa Demand among Bangladeshi Muslims (2025–2026)

Research note scope: what ordinary Bangladeshi Muslims ask Islamic Q&A services, how existing services answer, and the trust/authenticity problems. Research was limited (about 17 tool calls). Several target sites (alkawsar.com, at-tahreek.com) returned 404 at the guessed URLs, so a number of the ranking questions remain open. Gaps are flagged explicitly.

## Which topics dominate Bangla Islamic Q&A (ranking with real counts where available)

### Takeaway
The only hard view-count data found (IslamQA Bangla's "most read" list) shows that the **highest-demand questions are private, embarrassing purity and sexuality issues** (discharge types, masturbation), followed by eschatology (signs of the Day of Judgment), politics (democracy), and **divorce pronounced in anger**. People go to anonymous online Q&A for what they cannot comfortably ask an imam in person. The newest questions (Oct–Nov 2026) cover everyday purity, work/halal income (website design jobs) and transaction contracts, which confirms that tahara, income and family issues make up the daily stream.

### Cited Findings
- IslamQA Bangla (islamqa.info/bn) "সর্বাধিক পঠিত" (most-read) list, as of 4 Nov 2026 — [IslamQA Bangla home](https://islamqa.info/bn):
  1. Difference between semen, madhi and white vaginal discharge: **223,616 reads**
  2. Ruling on masturbation and how to avoid it: **222,721 reads**
  3. Minor and major signs of Qiyamah: **176,892 reads**
  4. Democracy in the Islamic view: **106,952 reads**
  5. Divorce given by an angry person: **70,781 reads**
- IslamQA Bangla's latest questions (Oct–Nov 2026) include: purity of a burqa that touched najasa; hunting for sport; whether website-design work is permissible; feeling something exit after istinja; when penalty clauses in contracts are valid — [IslamQA Bangla home](https://islamqa.info/bn)
- The IslamQA Bangla topic tree has 11 top-level categories. Family fiqh (পারিবারিক ফিকাহ) has the most subcategories (18), well ahead of Aqidah (6) and Hadith (4). There is also a separate category for "mental and social problems" (মানসিক ও সামাজিক সমস্যা) with 16 direct answers. The visible answer counts (18, 12, 10, 16…) appear to count only answers attached directly to the top-level node, not the full archive, so they should **not** be used to size the archive — [IslamQA Bangla topics](https://islamqa.info/bn/categories/topics)
- IslamQA Bangla also carries bid'ah rulings on Milad, e.g. a ruling on eating food distributed on Milad-un-Nabi, which it calls a later innovation — [IslamQA Bangla answer 89693](https://islamqa.info/bn/answers/89693)
- Bid'ah controversy remains live in 2026 mainstream media. A Jugantor (Aug 2026) piece covers a viewer asking Shaykh Ahmadullah on a Q&A programme whether calling Milad a bid'ah makes someone a kafir — [Jugantor](https://www.jugantor.com/islam-life/1144295). Jugantor (Feb 2026) on Shab-e-Barat hadith (citing al-Albani) — [Jugantor](https://www.jugantor.com/islam-life/1061122). Daily Inqilab (Feb 2025, Mufti Piyar Mahmud) argues the Shab-e-Barat narrations have collective support — [Inqilab](https://dailyinqilab.com/religion-philosophy/article/732264). An unattributed teachers.gov.bd blog (Jan 2026) argues most Shab-e-Barat hadith are individually weak — [teachers.gov.bd](https://teachers.gov.bd/index.php/blog/details/835934)
- Live TV and Facebook Q&A programmes with celebrity scholars (e.g. Shaykh Ahmadullah) are a major format. One Bangladeshi cleric's fatwa against using the Facebook "haha" emoji to mock people drew millions of views and international coverage, a sign of how far social-media fatwas travel — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1882546/media); [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/250454/bangladeshi-cleric-issues-fatwa-on-facebook-emoji)
- Monthly Alkawsar (Markazud Dawah Al-Islamia, Dhaka; published since 2005) runs a recurring evidence-backed Q&A section in every issue. Print issues sell for about 30 taka (Sept 2024 issue) — [Rokomari Sept 2024](https://www.rokomari.com/book/427979/monthly-alkawsar-september-2024); [Rokomari Jan 2024](https://www.rokomari.com/book/367971/masik-alkawsar-january-2024)
- At-Tahreek (Ahle Hadith, linked to Hadith Foundation Bangladesh, since 1997) describes itself as spreading knowledge based on Quran and sahih hadith, with its own fatwa board publishing Q&A from reliable references — [Brandfetch profile of at-tahreek.com](https://brandfetch.com/at-tahreek.com) (aggregator; primary site not reached)
- The Muslim Bangla app (TopOfStack Software Ltd) lists "Ask Scholars (Q&A)" alongside Quran, prayer times, hadith, zakat, matrimony and a marketplace — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id878084956); [Muslim Bangla blog](https://muslimbangla.com/blog/1013)

### Inferences
- Demand ranking (inferred from the IslamQA read counts plus category structure and media coverage; not a measured cross-platform ranking): (1) tahara, ghusl and sexual purity, including menstruation and discharge; (2) marriage, divorce and family, where angry or conditional talaq is a recurring crisis question; (3) halal income, jobs and transactions (bank jobs, interest, freelancing, contracts); (4) aqidah and eschatology (signs of Qiyamah, Dajjal); (5) bid'ah controversies, which peak seasonally around Shab-e-Barat, Milad and Muharram; (6) salah details; (7) politics and current affairs. Zakat and Qurbani questions are probably highly seasonal spikes, but no counts were found.
- The top two IslamQA questions are about shame-laden private matters. An app's "ask" feature should therefore offer **anonymous asking** and private history, and should not, by default, show user questions in public feeds under real names.
- A recurring "mental and social problems" category, plus divorce-in-anger as a top read, suggests that many questions are really distress calls. A good flow needs empathetic triage and a route to counselling, not only a hukm.
- Seasonal spikes (Shab-e-Barat, Ramadan, Qurbani, zakat, Milad) are natural daily or seasonal re-engagement hooks: pre-written, evidence-graded explainers can be surfaced before each event.

### Gaps
- No question counts were obtained for alkawsar.com, at-tahreek.com, ihadis, IslamHouse Bangla, or the Muslim Bangla app Q&A. The guessed URLs (alkawsar.com/bn/question-answer, at-tahreek.com/question_answer) returned 404, and search did not surface the correct Q&A index pages. The report writer should treat cross-platform ranking as unmeasured.
- No data on how many Facebook masala/Q&A pages exist, their follower counts, or their most-asked topics.
- No data found on Hadith Foundation phone-hotline volumes or answer turnaround times.
- IslamQA Bangla's full archive size was not shown on the pages fetched.

## What amal/wazifa people look for, and how much is unauthentic

### Takeaway
Wazifa demand centres on worldly problems: rizq and poverty, jobs, marriage, debt, illness, protection from jinn and black magic. The most-shared "amal" often rests on weak or fabricated narrations. The classic example is "recite Surah Waqi'ah nightly and you will never face poverty", whose chain hadith critics rate as severely weak (Imam Ahmad called it munkar). Real-world harm is documented: 2026 Bangladeshi news reports fake kabiraj and jinn-exorcism scams involving fraud, rape and killing, some run through online accounts (Imo).

### Cited Findings
- The popular Surah Waqi'ah-for-rizq narration has a severely weak chain. Its narrator Shuja'/Abu Shuja' is unknown, and Imam Ahmad reportedly said "I do not know him" / graded it munkar. So it cannot ground a guarantee of provision — [Tohed thread](https://en.tohed.com/threads/whoever-recites-surah-al-waqi%CA%BFah-nightly-will-not-face-poverty.3950/latest); [Tohed thread 2](https://en.tohed.com/threads/claim-of-surah-al-waqiah-ending-poverty-proven-fabricated.9064/latest) (Salafi-leaning forum; secondary)
- Many invented nafl prayers (special-night or special-purpose salah) are fabricated "in our times" and promoted as a means of nearness to Allah — [Tohed, from "Ghayr Masnun Nafli Namazayn"](https://en.tohed.com/threads/invalidity-of-innovated-voluntary-prayers-in-islam.11929)
- A Bangla text on hadithbd.com explains that the scholars' allowance to act on weak hadith in fada'il was misunderstood, and this let fabricated hadith spread in society — [hadithbd.com book PDF](https://www.hadithbd.com/books/pdf/?id=4671)
- Bogura, May 2026: a man practising kabiraji/jhar-phuk was arrested for raping an SSC examinee under the pretext of jinn exorcism — [Bangladesh Pratidin](https://www.bd-pratidin.com/country-village/2026/05/07/1247772)
- Jamalpur/Sherpur, April 2026: PBI arrested a youth who took gold and cash from a teenage girl in the name of kabiraji. Accomplices contacted her through several Imo accounts promising to "solve all problems", and the "Khurshed Kabiraj" persona did not exist — [bdnews24](https://bangla.bdnews24.com/samagrabangladesh/d36f5f84c858); [Bangladesh Pratidin](https://www.bd-pratidin.com/country/2026/04/16/1239580)
- A fake kabiraj couple was detained for killing a housewife through physical abuse during "jinn removal" (date unclear in the result) — [Jugantor](https://www.jugantor.com/country-news/190102)
- Bangladesh's High Court (Oct 2026) ordered six fake inflammatory videos removed from Facebook/YouTube. The order was not religious-amal specific, but it shows that misinformation on these platforms is recognised at court level — [bdnews24](https://bangla.bdnews24.com/bangladesh/jrxfjnc7qw)

### Inferences
- Wazifa search intent clusters by **problem**, not by surah. Users type "রিযিক বাড়ানোর আমল", "চাকরির জন্য দোয়া", "বিয়ের আমল", "ঋণ মুক্তির দোয়া", "পরীক্ষার দোয়া", "জাদু/বদনজর থেকে বাঁচার আমল". A feature should be organised by need-based entry points, each mapped to authentic masnun duas: for example the debt dua from Abu Dawud, istighfar for rizq (Nuh 71:10–12), morning and evening adhkar, the last two verses of al-Baqarah, and Ayat al-Kursi for protection. Each should show an explicit grade.
- Because the most viral "amal" are weak or fabricated, an authenticity label on every item (Sahih / Hasan / Da'if / Mawdu' / "no basis", with grader named) is the main differentiator. So is a **"myth-check" entry** for viral claims ("Is the Waqi'ah-for-rizq hadith authentic?"). This answers what people actually see on Facebook and YouTube.
- The ruqyah/kabiraj scam pattern (online persona, promises to solve everything, money and gold extraction, sexual abuse) argues for in-app safety copy: Shar'i ruqyah is self-recitation of Quran and masnun duas, needs no payment for "taweez", and never requires being alone with a stranger. The app should also avoid listing or brokering paid raqis.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measure was found of what share of Bangla Facebook/YouTube wazifa content is weak or fabricated.
- No specific Bangladeshi news story (2024–2026) was found that focuses on fake wazifa *content* (as opposed to kabiraj fraud).
- No data on dhikr-count search demand (e.g. "1000 times X").
- How ihadis, hadithbd or IslamHouse visually label hadith grades was not verified this session.

## How services handle madhhab differences and Hanafi vs Ahle Hadith disputes

### Takeaway
Bangla Q&A providers are mostly **school-aligned rather than comparative**. Alkawsar/Markazud Dawah is Deobandi-Hanafi, At-Tahreek/Hadith Foundation is Ahle Hadith, and IslamQA is Salafi and often categorical, e.g. calling Milad haram/bid'ah. Facebook gives preachers incentives to *heighten* sectarian rhetoric, which suggests that a neutral, multi-view product would fill a real gap but must be designed carefully.

### Cited Findings
- Hasan & Anoraga (2025), "Uniting or dividing? Digital sermons and sectarian debates among Muslim preachers on Facebook in Bangladesh", *Digital Muslim Review* 2(1). This netnography of waz videos by Dr. Muzaffar bin Mohsin (Salafi/Ahle Hadith), Mufti Rezaul Karim Abrar and Mufti Gias Uddin Taheri found that Facebook incentivises preachers to intensify sectarian rhetoric to boost engagement and authority, deepening divisions among Salafis, Deobandi Hanafis and shrine-based Sunnis — [UIII repository](https://repository.uiii.ac.id/items/deaf18fb-5726-4ed9-bafc-6fca5bd9de65)
- IslamQA Bangla takes a categorical stance on Milad (no "Eid-e-Milad" in Shari'ah, a post-salaf innovation) — [IslamQA 89693](https://islamqa.info/bn/answers/89693). Hanafi-leaning media (Inqilab) argue the opposite on Shab-e-Barat — [Inqilab](https://dailyinqilab.com/religion-philosophy/article/732264)
- Mainstream popular scholars field "is the other side kafir?" type questions on Q&A shows. The Ahmadullah Milad question shows that users want de-escalation answers — [Jugantor](https://www.jugantor.com/islam-life/1144295)
- Academic background: scholarly views on mawlid range from "good innovation" to outright bid'ah — [Noora Kamel, academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/9321153)
- At-Tahreek frames its identity as "Quran and sahih hadith" based — [Brandfetch](https://brandfetch.com/at-tahreek.com)

### Inferences
- Bangladesh is majority Hanafi with a vocal Ahle Hadith/Salafi minority online. The flashpoint questions are predictable: raf' al-yadayn, loud amin, reciting Fatiha behind the imam, 8 vs 20 rakat tarawih, women's salah posture, three talaq in one sitting (a major practical issue for the "divorce in anger" demand), Shab-e-Barat, Milad, and the timing of witr and Asr.
- A trustworthy design pattern: a default ruling shown per the user's chosen madhhab (Hanafi default for Bangladesh), with an expandable "অন্যান্য মত" (other views) panel. That panel lists each school's position and evidence, and tags each as an accepted ikhtilaf (valid difference) where that is the mainstream scholarly view. The tone should never be "the other side is wrong". This reduces alienation compared with single-school sites.
- Triple talaq deserves special handling. Hanafi and Ahle Hadith answers differ in outcome (three divorces vs one), and the stakes are real. The app should escalate to a human mufti rather than auto-answer.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: whether alkawsar.com or at-tahreek.com answers explicitly state madhhab, cite other views, or give turnaround times.
- No user-survey data on whether Bangladeshi users prefer single-madhhab or comparative answers.

## Evidence on AI/chatbot fatwa use and scholars' positions (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
No Bangladesh-specific reporting or scholarly statement on AI fatwa was found in this session. Internationally, the clearest 2026 position is Egypt's Dar al-Ifta (Jan 2026). It ruled that independent use of AI tools such as ChatGPT to interpret or derive meanings of the Quran is impermissible, because sources are uncertain, accuracy cannot be verified and there is no scholarly method. It still endorses digital tools generally, and itself runs a fatwa app (2022) and Ramadan AI-animated content (2026). Academic testing (2025) found no chatbot reached "trusted educational output" status.

### Cited Findings
- Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, Jan 2026: AI-based Quran interpretation ruled impermissible. Reasons given: AI uses statistical models without true understanding, has unverifiable sources and lacks scholarly methodology. Users are directed to established tafsir, qualified scholars and institutions. The ruling answered a citizen's question — [Aaj English](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330451560); [ProPakistani, 28 Jan 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/01/28/fatwa-issued-against-use-of-ai-for-quran-tafseer/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/can-muslims-use-ai-to-interpret-holy-quran-1.500422462); [Middle East AI News](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/egypts-dar-al-ifta-bans-ai-for-quran)
- The ban covers Quranic interpretation, not digital tools generally. Dar al-Ifta launched a mobile fatwa app in 2022 — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/can-muslims-use-ai-to-interpret-holy-quran-1.500422462). It also released its first AI-animated series for Ramadan 2026 — [Scene Now](https://scenenow.com/Buzz/Dar-Al-Ifta-Is-Dropping-Its-First-AI-Animated-Series-This-Ramadan). UAE and Egypt are exploring AI to modernise fatwa *delivery* — [Technave](https://technave.com/gadget/UAE-and-Egypt-explore-AI-to-modernise-fatwa-delivery-43766.html)
- Al-Ahram commentary on religious guidance in the AI age — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/549364.aspx)
- 2025 study, *Journal of Digital Islamicate Research* 3(1), "Artificial Intelligence in Islamic Guidance: Assessing Chatbot Performance and Jurisprudential Adherence": no chatbot achieved "Trusted Educational Output" status, citing the difficulty of verifying religious knowledge (full text not accessible, 403) — [DOI 10.1163/27732363-20250003](https://doi.org/10.1163/27732363-20250003)
- Middle East Forum argues that AI shifts religious authority from the producing scholar to the distributing platform — [MEF](https://www.meforum.org/mef-observer/who-controls-the-fatwa-in-the-ai-age) (think tank with a political orientation; opinion)
- Commentary: AI can retrieve and summarise but lacks the knowledge and judgment needed for ifta — [Amau Academy](https://library.amauacademy.com/ai-chatbots-for-religious-questions-useful-tool-or-dangerous-shortcut/)
- Training scholars to use ChatGPT for fatwa *research and writing* is offered (Al Balagh Academy, not Bangladesh-specific) — [Al Balagh](https://www.albalaghacademy.org/course/gpt-and-ai-for-islamic-education/)

### Inferences
- The defensible stance for a Bangladeshi app: AI may **search, retrieve and summarise existing, attributed fatwas and graded hadith** (a retrieval layer with citations), but must not *generate* novel rulings or tafsir. Novel or high-stakes questions (talaq, inheritance shares, riba in a specific contract) should be routed to named human muftis. This matches the Dar al-Ifta line between interpretation and tool-assisted access.
- Labelling answers as "AI summary of [named source]" versus "Answer by Mufti X, [institute]" will likely matter for trust, given scholars' public scepticism.

### Gaps
- No Bangladeshi scholar, Islamic Foundation Bangladesh, Qawmi board (Befaq) or Hadith Foundation statement on AI fatwa or chatbots was found. A targeted Bangla search ("এআই ফতোয়া", "চ্যাটজিপিটি দিয়ে ফতোয়া") is recommended.
- No usage data on how many Bangladeshis ask ChatGPT or Gemini religious questions.
- The original Arabic text of the Dar al-Ifta ruling was not retrieved.

## Features that would make a Q&A/ruling search trustworthy

### Takeaway
Based on the demand and failure modes above, trust depends on: visible evidence with hadith grades; a named scholar or institute for every answer; madhhab-aware defaults with an "other views" panel; myth-check of viral amal; anonymity for sensitive questions; and clear escalation to a human mufti for high-stakes personal cases. Retrieval of existing vetted answers is far safer than AI-generated rulings.

### Cited Findings
- Scholars and institutions cite unverifiable sources and no scholarly method as the core AI risk, so citation and attribution answer that risk directly — [Gulf News on Dar al-Ifta](https://gulfnews.com/technology/can-muslims-use-ai-to-interpret-holy-quran-1.500422462)
- The weak-hadith-in-fada'il misunderstanding is a known channel through which fabricated hadith spread, so grade labels address a real failure mode — [hadithbd.com](https://www.hadithbd.com/books/pdf/?id=4671)
- Sectarian incentives on Facebook push toward polarising answers — [Hasan & Anoraga 2025](https://repository.uiii.ac.id/items/deaf18fb-5726-4ed9-bafc-6fca5bd9de65)
- Existing trusted print and web providers present answers "with reference to evidence" (Alkawsar Q&A) — [Rokomari listing](https://www.rokomari.com/book/427979/monthly-alkawsar-september-2024). The Muslim Bangla app already markets "Ask Scholars (Q&A)", so it is the direct competitor benchmark — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id878084956)
- The most-read questions are private sexual and purity matters — [IslamQA Bangla](https://islamqa.info/bn)

### Inferences (feature recommendations for the founder)
1. **Evidence block on every answer**: Quran ayah (with Bangla translation and a tafsir link), hadith with collection, number and **grade + grader** (e.g. "সহীহ – আলবানী" / "হাসান – শুআইব আরনাউত"), and a link to the full hadith text.
2. **Attribution**: named mufti or institute (e.g. Markazud Dawah, Darul Uloom, Hadith Foundation, IslamQA), date answered and madhhab tag. No anonymous rulings.
3. **Madhhab setting** (Hanafi default) plus an "মতভেদ" panel showing each view respectfully and marking valid ikhtilaf.
4. **Amal by need** (rizq, job, marriage, debt, illness, exam, protection, nazar, sleep or fear). Only masnun or authentic items, each with a grade badge, plus a "ভাইরাল আমল যাচাই" (verify viral amal) search for Facebook claims.
5. **Ruqyah safety module**: self-ruqyah with authentic verses, and scam warnings drawn from documented cases (no payment for taweez, no private sessions with strangers, Imo/phone "kabiraj" red flags).
6. **Anonymous ask** with private history, and gentle triage for distress-type questions (divorce, marital conflict, waswasa), including links to counselling.
7. **Escalation**: auto-route talaq (especially triple talaq or talaq in anger), inheritance calculations, specific bank or insurance contracts, and anything personal and consequential to a human mufti queue, with a stated SLA.
8. **AI role limited to search and summarisation** of vetted answers, labelled as such, never generating new fatwa or tafsir. This is consistent with the Dar al-Ifta 2026 position.
9. **Seasonal pre-emption**: push graded explainers before Shab-e-Barat, Ramadan, Qurbani, zakat season and Milad. These are predictable demand spikes and daily-return hooks.

### Gaps
- No user research (survey or interviews) was found on which trust signals Bangladeshi users actually notice or value.
- Response-time benchmarks for Bangla human-mufti services (Muslim Bangla Q&A, Alkawsar, At-Tahreek, Hadith Foundation hotline) were not found.
- Whether Muslim Bangla's scholar Q&A shows citations, grades or scholar names was not verified.
