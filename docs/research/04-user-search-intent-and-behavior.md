# Users search to act, then verify

Research date: 5 November 2026. This report explains why Bangladeshi Muslims search for each major Islamic topic: the problem behind the search, what they want, how they want it, what makes them trust or leave a source, and what brings them back. It builds on the query data in [03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md](03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md) and the user-voice corpus summarised in [06-bangladesh-specific-user-needs.md](06-bangladesh-specific-user-needs.md). Gaps and opportunities are scored in [07-content-and-feature-gaps.md](07-content-and-feature-gaps.md), and timing is covered in [08-seasonal-search-trends.md](08-seasonal-search-trends.md). Row-level data: [data/keyword-database.csv](data/keyword-database.csv).

## Key findings

- **Most searches start from a practical need, not curiosity.** The commonest triggers are reciting a specific surah, saying the right dua for a situation, praying correctly at the right local time, checking whether something is allowed, or posting something on Facebook. The query names the object and the format it is wanted in ("সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ", Surah Yasin in Bangla pronunciation).
- **The core content format is Arabic, Bangla pronunciation (উচ্চারণ) and Bangla meaning side by side, with audio to check pronunciation by ear.** Users who cannot read Arabic say so directly in reviews. They ask for উচ্চারণ for duas as well as surahs, and they want audio.
- **Trust comes from four things:** a named source (Islamic Foundation, Al-Kawsar, Shaykh Ahmadullah), a named grading and reference, text with no errors, and an answer that matches the user's own madhhab expectations. Users type trusted names into their queries and report text errors verse by verse.
- **The top reasons users leave are ads around sacred text, paywalls on religious content, wrong prayer times and alarms that fail, and errors in Quran or hadith text.** Ads are the largest complaint theme in 11,671 Play reviews: 551 matches, 349 of them rated 3★ or lower. Users frame indecent ads as a religious objection ("নাজায়েজ", impermissible), not just bad UX.
- **Users come back for daily prayer times, a small daily Quran or dua portion that builds into a khatam, Friday-specific content, and Ramadan, which is when many first adopt an app.** Notifications cut both ways: users ask for reminders and complain about nagging in roughly equal measure.
- **Verification is a quiet need across every category.** Examples are "সঠিক উচ্চারণ" (correct pronunciation), "কোন সূরার আয়াত" (which surah is this ayah from), look-ups of hadith numbers, and "দলিলসহ" (with evidence). Users rarely search for authenticity as a topic. They want it built into the answer.

## How to read the tags

Every claim in the category tables carries one tag. **[Measured]** means a number: an Ahrefs volume bucket, a Google Trends ratio against the prayer-time anchor, or our own keyword-match count over the review corpus. **[Observed user feedback]** means users' own words. These are verbatim Google Play reviews (11,671 reviews of 22 Islamic apps on the BD storefront, scraped 5 Nov 2026) or the queries users type, as captured in autocomplete and results-page snapshots. Both show what users actually say or do, but neither has volume. **[Inferred]** means the analyst's interpretation. Play reviews cannot verify a reviewer's country, so this report leans on Bangla-script reviews and flags English reviews of global apps as "location unknown" ([Play: Muslim Bangla](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tos.namajtime&hl=bn&gl=BD)).

## Quran: reading a specific surah without reading Arabic

A large share of Quran searches comes from people who want to **recite a particular surah for a particular reason**, but cannot read Arabic script fluently. The surahs with the highest measured demand (Mulk, Kahf, Yasin, Rahman, Ayatul Kursi) are the ones tied to routines: Mulk at night, Kahf on Friday, Yasin for the dying and on special nights.

## Hadith: something to share, or a citation to check

Hadith searches split three ways. The biggest group, by measured volume, wants **a short hadith on a topic** to post, quote or use in school. Ahrefs puts several short-hadith list queries above 1,000 a month. A second group **checks a numbered citation** they saw elsewhere. A third arrives seasonally, for example looking for the virtue of Shab-e-Barat. Browsing whole collections is a small activity on Google.

## Dua and zikir: a life situation, often on someone else's behalf

Dua is the largest year-round Islamic search family. Its averages run about 2.5x the prayer-time anchor ([Trends T9](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=BD&q=%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC,%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE,%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%95%20%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE,%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A6%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B8,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%95%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AB)). The long tail is organised by **problem**: sleep, illness, debt, anxiety, exams, a parent who has died. It is often typed for someone else.

## Namaz and prayer times: the right minute, for my district, my madhhab

Prayer times are the most habitual search. The term never drops to zero in any week, and in earlier research it peaked at 04:00–06:00, before and at Fajr ([Trends hourly](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=BD&date=now%207-d)). Accuracy is judged to the minute, against the Islamic Foundation and against Hanafi practice.

| Dimension | Finding | Tag and source |

## Worked example: the journey behind "সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ"

Take one high-demand query and follow it end to end. "সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ" (Surah Yasin, Bangla pronunciation) sits in Ahrefs' **>1,000 a month bucket with KD Easy**. So do its siblings: "সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ সহ অনুবাদ" (with translation), "সূরা ইয়াসিন লিখিত" (written), "সূরা ইয়াসিন সম্পূর্ণ" (complete) and "সূরা ইয়াসিন আরবি pdf" ([Ahrefs সূরা ইয়াসিন](https://ahrefs.com/keyword-generator/?country=bd&input=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8)). [Measured]

**The trigger.** The reversed-order term "ইয়াসিন সূরা" reached its five-year peak in **Shab-e-Barat week 2023** (5–11 Mar), not in Ramadan ([Trends quran set A](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=BD&date=today%205-y&q=%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC,%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%AE%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%95,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8,%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A4%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B2%20%E0%A6%95%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF)). [Measured] That fits the Bangladeshi practice of reciting Yasin on special nights and for the dying and the deceased. [Inferred] The person searching is often doing so for an occasion or a family moment, and many cannot read the Arabic. A kutblog reviewer puts it plainly: "যারা আরবি জানে না বা বৃদ্ধ,বয়স হয়ে গেছে আরবি শিখা তো সম্ভব না… এই বাংলাতে যেন অর্থসহ পড়ে" (those who don't know Arabic, or are old, can't learn it… let them read it in Bangla with meaning) ([Play: kutblog](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kutblog.arabicbanglaquran&hl=bn&gl=BD)). [Observed user feedback]

**The query and its modifiers.** Autocomplete shows users narrowing by format: লিখিত (written, as opposed to video), আরবি pdf, বাংলা উচ্চারণ সহ pdf, ফজিলত (virtue), and "ইয়াসিন সূরা কত পারায়" (which para is Yasin in). On YouTube they look for "সূরা ইয়াসিন তেলাওয়াত মধুর কন্ঠে" (recited in a sweet voice) ([Autocomplete সূরা ইয়াসিন](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=bn&gl=bd&q=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8); [YouTube autocomplete](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&ds=yt&hl=bn&gl=bd&q=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8)). [Observed user feedback]

**What the results page offers.** On Google (gl=bd), the page opens with **an AI-generated answer**, then a **video carousel**, then muslimbangla.com and channel7tvbd.com, a TV channel's site. DuckDuckGo-BD shows alqurans.com, two informationbangla.com pages, and news portals (dhakapost, kalbela) ([Google SERP](https://www.google.com/search?q=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8%20%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%82%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%89%E0%A6%9A%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%A3&gl=bd&hl=bn)). [Observed user feedback] Bangla-script pronunciation is rare among the dedicated sites audited. Of those, only muslimbangla shows it per verse, and quran.com offers Latin transliteration only ([05](05-competitor-feature-analysis.md)). The user lands either on an AI summary of uncertain accuracy, a video, or an ad-funded news or blog page.

**Where the journey breaks.** Three failure modes recur in reviews. The first is **errors in the very surah being read**. A PG Apps BD reviewer gave 1★ because the last ayah of Yasin read "কুল্লা" where it should be "কুল্লি": "এই এপবা কোরআন কেউ ডাউনলোড করবেননা এড আসে খুব আবার ভুলো দেওয়া আচে" (nobody download this Quran app: lots of ads, and there are mistakes too) ([Play: PG Apps BD](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pgappsbd.all_quran_orthosho&hl=bn&gl=BD)). The second is **ads breaking concentration mid-surah** ([Play: kutblog](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kutblog.arabicbanglaquran&hl=bn&gl=BD)). The third is **font too small for older readers**: "বাংলা অনুবাদে ব‍্যবহৃত বর্নের ফন্ট এক ধাপ বড় হলে বয়স্ক পাঠকের জন্য অধিকতর সুবিধা হত" (a one-step-larger Bangla font would help elderly readers) ([Play: PG Apps BD](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pgappsbd.all_quran_orthosho&hl=bn&gl=BD)). A further subtle failure is transliteration that does not match the translation, or that renders sounds wrongly. One reviewer with 161 helpful votes asked for colloquial rather than sadhu Bangla ([Play: kutblog](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kutblog.arabicbanglaquran&hl=bn&gl=BD)). [Observed user feedback]

**What would make them stay and return.** [Inferred] A single Surah Yasin page should load fast and text-first on a budget Android phone. It would show Arabic, proofread Bangla উচ্চারণ and a named Bangla translation verse by verse. It would offer synced audio as a pronunciation check and a large font by default. It would include the virtue with its grading, a print/PDF view for the people asking for "লিখিত" and "pdf", "which para" answered on the page, and a visible "report an error" link with a public corrections log. The return path is the routine around the surah: the same page bookmarked for the next Shab-e-Barat, or a nudge toward the Thursday-night and Friday routines (Kahf), without nagging notifications. Reviews of Muslims Day, kutblog and Greentech complain explicitly about repeated notification prompts ([Play: Muslims Day](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=theoaktroop.appoframadan&hl=bn&gl=BD)). [Observed user feedback]

## What changes across categories, and what does not

Two patterns hold across every category. First, **format modifiers are content requirements.** উচ্চারণ, অর্থসহ, pdf, ছবি, অডিও and "কত টাকা ২০২৬" each map to something a page must contain. A page missing any of them sends the user back to search ([03](03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md)). Second, **trust is assessed at the level of the individual item**: a verse, a hadith number, a prayer minute, a mufti's name. Our review corpus shows that local Bangla-first apps get 1–2★ in only about 2–5% of reviews, against 41% for Wemu and 19% for Muslim Pro ([Play: Wemu](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fyxtech.muslim&hl=bn&gl=BD); [Play: Muslim Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bitsmedia.android.muslimpro&hl=bn&gl=BD)). [Measured] Users reward accuracy and restraint more than breadth.

What changes is the **social direction** of the search. Quran, dua and namaz searches are for the searcher's own practice. Hadith, captions and status searches are for posting to others. Q&A and dream searches are private. [Inferred] Each needs a different default: practice pages need routines and progress, shareable pages need reference-carrying share cards, and private pages need anonymity and no social prompts.

## Confidence and limitations

Confidence is **high** that the needs listed exist. Each one is backed by users' own words, either in queries or in reviews, often both. Confidence is **medium** on how large each need is relative to the others. The volumes come from Ahrefs buckets (Quran, hadith and dua only) and Trends ratios, which are relative and exact-phrase only ([03](03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md)). Confidence is **lower** on leave and return reasons for **websites**. The richest evidence is Google Play reviews of apps, and website visitors who never install an app are absent from the corpus. Review samples are the newest and "most relevant" reviews, not a random sample. Theme counts come from keyword matching and are indicative only. Reviewers' countries cannot be verified. Reddit, Facebook groups and YouTube comments could not be retrieved, so trust signals such as "রেফারেন্স দিন" (please give a reference) in comments are unmeasured. There is no retention, DAU or session data for any BD Islamic site or app. Statements about what brings users back rest on reviews and search timing, not on observed return visits. Hourly patterns (Fajr and dream peaks) come from earlier research (Nov 4 2026) and were not re-pulled. Lifestyle leave-reasons are inferred, with no direct user evidence. See [10-research-methodology-and-sources.md](10-research-methodology-and-sources.md) for methods.
