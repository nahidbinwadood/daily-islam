# Bangladesh's Islamic web runs on mobile search

**Key findings**

- **The audience is large but harder to count than it looks.** About 91% of Bangladesh's ~170–176M people are Muslim (BBS Census 2022). Unique internet users are **82.8M** (DataReportal/Kepios, end-2025), while BTRC counts **135.94M internet subscriptions** (June 2026). The first counts people, the second counts SIMs and accounts. A rough Muslim online audience of **~75M people** is an estimate (91% × 82.8M), not a sourced figure.
- **Access is mobile.** About **89% of internet subscriptions are mobile** (BTRC), and Android is about 91% of mobile OS share. StatCounter shows a desktop majority (56.04%) for October 2026. That figure conflicts with every other signal, so we treat it as an artefact of StatCounter's sample.
- **Data is cheap per GB but not free, and connections are mid-speed.** Retail mobile data costs about **BDT 25–35 per GB**, and median mobile download speed is **36.84 Mbps** (Ookla via DataReportal). There is a large **gender gap**: 40% of men and 24% of women use mobile internet (GSMA 2024).
- **Islamic content reaches Bangladeshi users through four channels at once:** dedicated Bangla Islamic sites, the Islam sections of national newspapers, YouTube and Facebook, and apps. Dedicated sites get **48–66% of their desktop visits from organic search**. YouTube appears in **9 of 16** sampled Bing-BD results pages, and Facebook appears in most fiqh results pages.
- **The landscape splits by school of thought as well as by format.** Hanafi/Deobandi-leaning sites (alkawsar.com, muslimbangla.com) and Ahl-e-Hadith/Salafi-leaning sites (at-tahreek.com, tawheederdak, IRD's ihadis.com, hadithbd.com's audience cluster) compete for the same queries. The government sites (Islamic Foundation, quran.gov.bd) barely function as products.
- **Google search interest in core Islamic terms fell 30–60% from 2023 to 2026.** For example, the prayer-time anchor term fell 34% and আয়াতুল কুরসি (Ayatul Kursi) fell 57%. Meanwhile every sampled Google results page showed an **AI-generated answer** (6/6), and 5 of 6 showed a **YouTube video block**. Two things are inferred, not measured: the cause of the decline, and whether demand is moving to AI answers, video and apps.

## Ninety-one percent of a 170-million nation is Muslim, but only about half is online

Bangladesh's 2022 census found **91.04% Muslim** in the preliminary report and **91.08%** in the final national report. The rest were Hindu 7.96%, Buddhist 0.61%, Christian 0.30% and others 0.06%. Applied to the post-enumeration adjusted total of about 169.8M, that is roughly **154.5M (15.45 crore) Muslims** ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/hindu-population-bangladesh-decreases-further-466170); [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/bbs-bmet-data-shows-stark-contrast-3481341); [Wikipedia – 2022 Bangladeshi census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangladeshi_census)). DataReportal uses the UN estimate of **176M people** (October 2025) ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). The difference between 169.8M and 176M is a difference between sources and dates, not an error.

Internet reach depends heavily on what is counted. **DataReportal/Kepios counts 82.8M unique internet users (47.0% penetration)** at the end of 2025, up 5.3M (+6.9%) in a year. That leaves **93.4M people (53.0%) offline**, and there are 64.0M social-media user identities. Kepios itself warns that data lags may make the user figures too low ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). The regulator BTRC reports **135.94M internet subscriptions in June 2026**: 120.84M mobile internet and 15.10M broadband (ISP+PSTN). Subscriptions had dipped to a three-year low of 128.27M in February before recovering by +6.95M from January to June ([Financial Express – H1 growth](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/internet-subscriber-growth-outpaces-mobile-connections-in-h1); [The Peninsula](https://www.thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/01/09/2026/bangladesh-adds-over-6-mln-internet-users-in-h1); [Financial Express – Feb low](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/internet-subscriptions-hit-3-yr-low-cell-phone-users-edge-up-in-feb)). These BTRC figures come through news reports, not from BTRC's own tables, and outlets report slightly different totals for the same months.

The two numbers measure different things. BTRC counts **subscriptions**: every active SIM with a data plan and every broadband account, so one person with two SIMs is counted twice. A SIM in a household phone shared by several people is counted once. GSMA likewise counts **186M cellular connections, equal to 105% of the population** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). That is how the number of connections can exceed the number of people. Kepios estimates **unique people**. For audience sizing, the 82.8M figure is the right base. The BTRC figure is the right one for the **channel mix**, and it says connectivity is mostly mobile. A third measure, from earlier research (Nov 4 2026), is BBS's own household survey, which reported **59.2% individual internet use** using a different method ([UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/mobile-phone-ownership-rises-bbs/195830)). So the true penetration likely sits between 47% and 59%.

Combining these gives an **estimated ~75M Muslim internet users**. The calculation is 91% × 82.8M, and it assumes Muslims and non-Muslims are online at the same rate. No source states this figure; it is our arithmetic.

## Phones carry the traffic, and StatCounter's desktop reading is an outlier

Every signal except one says Bangladeshi internet use is mobile. **About 89% of BTRC internet subscriptions are mobile** (June 2026). The operators' subscriber bases are Grameenphone 86.59M, Robi 58.57M, Banglalink 37.86M and Teletalk 6.80M ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/internet-subscriber-growth-outpaces-mobile-connections-in-h1)). **Android is about 91% of mobile OS share** ([StatCounter OS](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/bangladesh)). Budget handsets dominate. The following comes from earlier research (Nov 4 2026); the Daily Sun page returned 403 when re-fetched. In IDC's Q3 2025 shipments, TECNO had 20.4%, Infinix 18.2% and Xiaomi 14.9% ([Daily Sun](https://www.daily-sun.com/other/838292/value-for-money-redefines-bangladesh-s-smartphone-market)). In Q1 2024, sub-$100 phones were a third of the market ([iConnect007/Counterpoint](https://iconnect007.com/article/141343/bangladesh-mobile-phone-market-grew-17-in-the-first-quarter-of-2024/141340)).

**StatCounter's platform share for October 2026 shows desktop 56.04%, mobile 43.6% and tablet 0.35%** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/bangladesh)). We do not use this to claim Bangladeshis browse mainly on desktop. StatCounter measures page views on the sites that carry its tracker. That sample can tilt toward office and desktop-heavy sites, and it can include automated traffic. We did not pull the 2025–2026 StatCounter history, so we cannot tell whether this is a one-month spike or a trend. A related caution applies to the traffic data in [report 02](02-top-islamic-websites-bangladesh.md): **Similarweb's free traffic-source percentages are desktop-only**.

## Cheap gigabytes, mid-range speeds and a wide gender gap

Bonik Barta (September 2024) put **retail mobile data at about BDT 25–35 per GB**. Operators' cost was BDT 2.25–5.25 per GB, and customers say they feel "deprived and overcharged" ([Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/exclusive/Y1mr7cn0S586gKMz/)). Cable.co.uk's 2022 ranking had Bangladesh at about Tk 33 ($0.32) per GB, the 12th cheapest in the world. Sources disagree on the 2023 figure ($0.23 vs $0.70), and that conflict is unresolved ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/297107/bangladesh-has-cheap-but-slow-mobile-data)).

Speed figures vary by month and by how they are reported. DataReportal cites a **median mobile download of 36.84 Mbps and fixed 60.26 Mbps** (Ookla, 2025) ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). Ookla's own index readings, reported in the news, range from 24.59 Mbps (March 2024, 112th of 148) ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/bangladesh-drops-global-mobile-internet-speed-ranking-3597346)) to 41.72 Mbps (November 2025, against a global median of 103.01; from a search snippet) ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/infograph/numbers/fastest-mobile-internets-world-1337251)). We found no 2026 Ookla figure. The practical takeaway is that a heavy page still costs real money on a prepaid pack, and audio or video should stream only when the user asks for it. [Report 06](06-bangladesh-specific-user-needs.md) weighs how strongly users themselves raise this.

**The gender gap is among the widest in Asia.** GSMA data for 2024, reported by Dhaka Tribune, puts mobile-internet adoption at **40% for men and 24% for women**. A later comparison gives 26% for women, and the two figures have not been reconciled ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/bangladesh/346828/bangladesh-tops-asia-in-mobile-internet-gender-gap); [GSMA Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025](https://www.gsma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Mobile-Gender-Gap-Report-2025.pdf)). Earlier research (Nov 4 2026) noted that the social-media audience is about 63% male ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). Women are therefore a smaller and harder-to-reach part of the online audience. Report 06 shows that their specific feature needs are barely visible in the review evidence.

## Four channels share Islamic attention, and search feeds the dedicated sites

Islamic content reaches Bangladeshi users through four overlapping channels.

**Dedicated Bangla Islamic websites** are small by national standards but well defined. The two leaders, muslimbangla.com and hadithbd.com, each draw an estimated **0.6–0.9M Bangladesh visits a month**. Their Similarweb Bangladesh ranks are about #460–480 ([Similarweb muslimbangla.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/muslimbangla.com/); [Similarweb hadithbd.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hadithbd.com/)). Report 02 has the full ranking. **Organic search is the top channel for nearly every one of these sites, at 48–66% of desktop visits.** The exceptions are quraanshareef.org (60.62% direct) and islamijindegi.com (direct-led, app-linked). Social referrals come from Facebook, WhatsApp Web and YouTube ([Similarweb hadithbd.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hadithbd.com/); [Similarweb quraanshareef.org](https://www.similarweb.com/website/quraanshareef.org/)). The head searches are practical: **নামাজের সময়সূচি** ("prayer schedule", Ahrefs BD volume 37K) and **ফজরের নামাজের সময়** ("Fajr prayer time", 31K) ([Ahrefs muslimbangla.com](https://ahrefstop.com/websites/muslimbangla.com)). Next come duas, single-surah pages with Bangla meaning, Islamic baby names and seasonal worship. [Keyword research](03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md) covers demand in detail.

**National newspapers are one to two orders of magnitude bigger than any Islamic site.** prothomalo.com had about **20.0M implied Bangladesh visits in September 2026** (30.4M global × 65.82% BD) and is **Bangladesh's #10 site** ([Similarweb prothomalo.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/prothomalo.com/)). Free tools cannot measure how much of that traffic goes to its ধর্ম (religion) section. Even so, the papers clearly compete for Islamic queries. Jugantor publishes a dated "আজকের নামাজের সময়সূচি" (today's prayer schedule) article ([Jugantor](https://www.jugantor.com/islam-life)). Newspapers appear in many sampled results pages: prothomalo.com for সূরা কাহফ প্রথম ১০ আয়াত (first 10 ayat of Surah Kahf); jagonews24.com in 6 of 16 Bing-BD pages; dhakapost.com in 4. Similarweb also finds heavy audience overlap between at-tahreek.com and news portals: jagonews24 85%, prothomalo 83%, somoynews.tv 80% ([Similarweb at-tahreek.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/at-tahreek.com/)).

**YouTube and Facebook own listening and private questions.** On the six Google-BD results pages captured, **5 of 6 showed a YouTube video block** with view counts in the lakhs (hundreds of thousands) ([Google SERP সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ](https://www.google.com/search?q=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8%20%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%82%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%89%E0%A6%9A%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%A3&gl=bd&hl=bn)). **youtube.com appeared in 9 of 16 Bing-BD top-10s** ([Bing ঘুমানোর দোয়া](https://www.bing.com/search?q=%E0%A6%98%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE&cc=BD&setlang=bn)). Across 34 sampled Google-BD fiqh and practice queries, results pages for রুকইয়াহ (ruqyah), গান শোনা কি হারাম (is listening to music haram) and ঈদের নামাজের নিয়ম (how to pray Eid prayer) carried **33, 29 and 14 YouTube links**. Facebook posts and pages appeared in most fiqh results pages ([SERP রুকইয়াহ](https://www.google.com/search?hl=bn&gl=bd&q=রুকইয়াহ); [SERP গান শোনা কি হারাম](https://www.google.com/search?hl=bn&gl=bd&q=গান+শোনা+কি+হারাম)). Social media reaches **64.0M user identities** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)). Facebook groups and YouTube comments could not be retrieved, so we cannot say how much Islamic Q&A happens there. We can only say that those platforms rank in search.

**Apps are the habit layer.** The largest Bangla Islamic apps have millions of installs on Google Play. These are global, device-level counts: Greentech Al Quran 14.7M, kutblog Bangla Quran উচ্চারণসহ ("with pronunciation") 8.67M, Muslim Bangla 5.99M, Muslims Day 5.69M and IRD Al Hadith 3.04M ([Play: Muslim Bangla](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tos.namajtime&hl=bn&gl=BD); [Play: Greentech Al Quran](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.greentech.quran&hl=bn&gl=BD)). Several web leaders also run apps and push them: muslimbangla, ihadis, islamqa and hadithbd. quraanshareef.org and islamijindegi.com show the direct-traffic pattern typical of app companion sites. This document treats the web as the primary channel. The review evidence in report 06 nonetheless comes mostly from apps, because that is where Bangladeshi users write reviews.

Three features of this map matter for planning.

First, **the two schools of thought compete for the same answer**. On রাগের মাথায় তালাক দিলে কি তালাক হয় ("does a divorce pronounced in anger count?"), Google-BD showed muslimbangla, Ahle Haq Media, IslamQA, al-Itisam and at-Tahreek on one page. None of them labels its madhhab ([SERP](https://www.google.com/search?hl=bn&gl=bd&q=রাগের+মাথায়+তালাক+দিলে+কি+তালাক+হয়)). Autocomplete also shows users appending school labels to their queries, for example "বিতর নামাজের নিয়ম আহলে হাদিস" (how to pray witr, Ahl-e-Hadith) and "namaz time dhaka today hanafi" ([Autocomplete হানাফি](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=bn&gl=bd&q=হানাফি)).

Second, **the state is absent as a digital product.** The Islamic Foundation homepage carries notices, tenders and zakat-fund bank details, but no prayer timetable, Hijri calendar or moon-sighting notice. Its "Quran" links point to Google Drive files. quran.gov.bd is a 2012 build that serves verse text as images and links to Adobe Flash ([IF](https://islamicfoundation.gov.bd/); [quran.gov.bd](https://quran.gov.bd/)). Users nonetheless treat IF as the reference for prayer times (report 06), so third parties fill that gap.

Third, **the most-visited results for many dua and topic-hadith queries are not Islamic institutions.** They are news portals and ad-heavy blogs; [report 05](05-competitor-feature-analysis.md) covers their quality.

## Search interest is shrinking while AI answers and video take the top of the page

Google Trends Bangladesh, comparing yearly means on the same anchor scale, shows that **almost every core Quran, hadith and dua term declined from 2023 to 2026**:

| Term | 2023 → 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| নামাজের সময় (prayer time; the anchor) | 46.7 → 30.6 | −34% |
| আয়াতুল কুরসি (Ayatul Kursi) | 14.3 → 6.1 | −57% |
| ayatul kursi | 20.3 → 8.7 | −57% |
| surah mulk | 11.9 → 6.4 | −46% |
| surah rahman | 7.3 → 3.7 | −49% |
| সাইয়েদুল ইস্তেগফার (Sayyidul Istighfar) | 4.7 → 1.7 | −64% |
| বুখারী শরীফ (Bukhari Sharif) | 2.2 → 0.9 | −59% |
| quran bangla | 8.6 → 5.0 | −42% |

Exceptions were flat or rising: **বাংলা উচ্চারণ (Bangla pronunciation) 32.7 → 35.4**, **নামাজ (namaz) 16.3 → 17.0**, জিকির (zikir) 2.6 → 2.7 and hadis 6.5 → 6.8. The 2026 mean covers January to early November and includes Ramadan ([Trends quran set A](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=BD&date=today%205-y&q=%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC,%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%AE%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%95,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8,%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A4%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B2%20%E0%A6%95%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF); [Trends hadith set A](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=BD&date=today%205-y&q=%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC,%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A6%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B8,%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%80%E0%A6%B8,hadith,hadis)). These are **relative interest indices, not search counts**.

The most likely reason sits at the top of the results page. **All six Google-BD results pages captured showed an AI-generated answer.** For আয়াতুল কুরসি বাংলা উচ্চারণ (Ayatul Kursi Bangla pronunciation), the AI answer itself cited Prothom Alo, jagonews24.com and a Play Store app ([Google SERP সূরা ইয়াসিন বাংলা উচ্চারণ](https://www.google.com/search?q=%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%82%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%A8%20%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%82%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BE%20%E0%A6%89%E0%A6%9A%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9A%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%A3&gl=bd&hl=bn)). Five of the six also carried a YouTube block. Simple "give me the text of X" queries are exactly what an AI box can answer without a click. Three explanations for the decline are plausible: AI answers, a shift of listening to YouTube, and a shift of daily use to apps. The research notes label all three as **inference, not measurement**. A fourth possibility cannot be ruled out: Trends exact-phrase matching undercounts terms as users spread across more spellings.

There is one apparent contradiction. **Ahrefs' modelled organic traffic for muslimbangla.com grew roughly twelvefold over the same period**: March 2023 was 29.3K and March 2026 was 357.0K ([Ahrefs muslimbangla.com](https://ahrefs.com/websites/muslimbangla.com)). One reading is that a shrinking pool of clicks is concentrating on a few winners. The other is that Ahrefs' model differs from Trends. Both tools agree that **muslimbangla.com and hadithbd.com lost search traffic in the latest month**: −73.7K and −58.4K respectively in Ahrefs, with hadithbd also −9.6% in Similarweb ([Ahrefs hadithbd.com](https://ahrefstop.com/websites/hadithbd.com); [Similarweb hadithbd.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hadithbd.com/)). For a new entrant, this argues for **interactive value**: audio sync, counters, routines, tracking and verification. AI boxes cannot easily replace these, whereas a static text page competes directly with the AI box.

Seasonality layers on top of the decline. Individual Ramadan keywords swing **about 5–40×** ([Google Trends BD, Ramadan set](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=BD&q=%E0%A6%A8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC,%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%9C%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BF,%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%AB%E0%A6%A4%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0,%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B0%20%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%AE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC)). Site-level Ramadan lifts in Ahrefs' modelled organic traffic are only **about 1.2–1.9×**, because these sites carry year-round demand ([Ahrefs hadithbd.com](https://ahrefs.com/websites/hadithbd.com); [Ahrefs muslimbangla.com](https://ahrefs.com/websites/muslimbangla.com)). [Keyword research](03-bangladesh-keyword-research.md) and [seasonal search trends](08-seasonal-search-trends.md) cover this in depth.

## Conclusion

The Bangladeshi Islamic web is a mobile, search-led market of roughly 75 million potential users (our estimate). Two Bangla-first portals lead it, and newspapers, YouTube and apps press on it from three sides. What changed during this research is the picture of search itself. The channel that built today's leaders is shrinking on core reference terms, and Google is answering more of those queries directly. A new platform should treat SEO as necessary but declining. It should build daily-use utilities such as district prayer times, today's portion and trackers, which earn direct and returning visits, rather than relying on static text pages to win clicks. The gender gap and the government's absence from digital products are structural openings, but neither is yet backed by direct user demand in the evidence.

## Confidence & limitations

- **High confidence:** Muslim share (census); DataReportal and BTRC figures, with the caveat that BTRC is relayed through news; the mobile majority; the direction of the Trends decline, which is consistent across about 10 terms.
- **Medium confidence:** data price and speed (ranges, with conflicting sources); gender-gap magnitude (24% vs 26% unreconciled); channel shares, which are desktop-only Similarweb splits.
- **Low confidence / inference:** the ~75M Muslim online audience; madhhab orientation labels; causes of the search decline; StatCounter's desktop reading, which we discount. Newspaper Islam-section traffic is **unmeasured**.
- Google results pages were sampled thinly: 6 Quran queries before a CAPTCHA, plus 34 fiqh queries from a signed-in Chrome session that may be personalised. See [methodology](10-research-methodology-and-sources.md).
